# Market Data → Strategy

Market calculations do not equal strategy.

CR3/CR10/HHI describe the supplied sample's concentration. Price bands describe where observed transactions/listings cluster. A price valley is a hypothesis, not proof of unmet demand. Configuration efficiency is useful only after checking sample quality, demand, margin and shopper fit.

The strategy layer should translate market evidence into:
- entry posture;
- differentiation burden;
- price/offer hypotheses;
- SKU configuration hypotheses;
- competitor white-space questions;
- research still needed;
- implications for positioning, listing and asset architecture.

## Opportunity interpretation
Use an opportunity map that distinguishes observed signal from recommendation. A candidate opportunity requires demand evidence, product fit, economics, a platform route and a measurable hypothesis. Price valleys, low-competition keywords and weak clusters remain provisional until those cross-checks pass.

## Search + competitor connection
Search terms reveal entry questions; competitor clusters reveal the decision set. The strategy should explain whether the product wins by a better configuration, clearer value proposition, stronger proof, lower decision friction, different usage job or another evidence-backed route—not merely by looking different.

## Customer voice connection
Supplied competitor/review theme labels add the customer's lived decision language to structural market data. Aggregate explicit labels with source and audience provenance; keep unclassified records visible; never treat sample theme counts as population prevalence without representativeness evidence. A useful theme should flow through Product Truth fit before it becomes a Positioning tension, Listing objection/FAQ, platform-content hook or Dynamic Asset decision job.
