# Visual / Photography / CMF Strategy

## Purpose
Translate Product Truth, Positioning, Message Hierarchy and Consumer Decision Questions into a shoot-ready and generation-ready visual system. Visual decisions are commercial decisions with physical constraints.

## Positive-first principle
Plants, water, laboratory cues, models, hands, props, animals, lifestyle scenes, macro texture, diagrams and abstract materials are selectable tools. Use them only when `Product Truth + Consumer Question + Platform + Culture + Creative Strategy` gives them a job. Do not globally ban or require them.

## Visual territory
Explain:
- what perception the territory should create;
- which shopper belief it advances;
- how it differs from category clutter when market evidence exists;
- why it remains truthful to the real product;
- how it scales across gallery, video, creator, paid and PDP surfaces.

## Composition
Specify and justify:
- product occupancy;
- negative space;
- focal hierarchy;
- copy-safe / crop-safe zones;
- shot size;
- camera height;
- yaw / pitch / roll;
- foreground/background relationship.

The reason cannot be “looks premium”. Tie the geometry to product recognition, specification visibility, proof, usage understanding or platform crop behavior.

## Lens / perspective / working distance
- Long focal lengths can reduce perspective exaggeration and preserve packaging geometry for product-truth views.
- Wider lenses can show environment/action when context is the commercial job, but must not distort the product.
- Working distance is part of perspective and lighting/reflection control.
State why the chosen combination serves the exact asset.

## Focus / aperture / DOF
- Identity/evidence assets usually require critical product information to remain readable.
- Context/emotion assets may use selective focus when the blur guides attention without hiding required facts.
- Macro imagery must distinguish actual product texture references from abstract material metaphors.

## Lighting geometry
For key / fill / negative fill / rim / background light explain:
- direction and size;
- distance/relative softness;
- contrast intent;
- material response;
- label readability;
- skin-tone behavior when people appear.

Do not specify “soft light” without saying what it solves.

## CCT / WB / exposure / highlights
- First protect accurate product color and skin tone.
- Use CCT for atmosphere only after a reliable neutral/brand reference is established.
- Define highlight roll-off/clipping policy for white packs, glossy films, transparent containers, metal and reflective printing.

## Reflection / polarization
Use flags, diffusion, angle change and polarization to control reflections while retaining material identity. Removing all reflection can make glossy or transparent materials look fake; uncontrolled reflection can erase labels.

## CMF
Treat Color / Material / Finish as an engineering input:
- color affects contrast and reproduction tolerance;
- material affects transmission/reflection/scattering;
- finish affects specular size and highlight shape.
CMF must change lighting, background and post-production decisions.

## People / casting / styling
Models appear when they answer relevance, usage, scale or social-context questions.
- Preserve natural skin texture and plausible local representation.
- Consider skin tone, hair, age, clothing, body exposure, gestures, religion/festival context and family structure only when relevant evidence exists.
- Do not infer behavior from race/ethnicity.

## Shot list
Every shot records:
`asset_id → commercial job → consumer question → camera → composition → lighting → product fidelity → WHY → success metric`.
No shot exists merely because the package “needs another image”.
