# Positioning / Value Proposition / Message Architecture

## Purpose
Create one coherent reason to choose the product, then sequence the supporting beliefs. Do not turn the product fact list into a wall of equal-weight selling points.

## Positioning decision
State:
- target customer / decision context;
- JTBD or consumer tension;
- frame of reference / category;
- Primary Conversion Proposition;
- competitive alternative, including “do nothing / delay purchase” when relevant;
- differentiation source;
- reasons-to-believe;
- risk reducers;
- proof boundary.

The differentiation source should come from `Product Truth × Consumer Question × Market/Search/Competitor evidence × Offer feasibility`. If market evidence is missing, do not claim competitors lack a feature or that the product owns a whitespace.

## Value Proposition
Build the chain:
`Attribute / Fact → Advantage → Perceived Benefit → Psychosocial/Emotional Value → Evidence`.
- A fact may be commercially irrelevant.
- An advantage must explain what changes for the user.
- A benefit cannot be stronger than the approved claim/evidence.
- Emotional value is earned only when the functional chain genuinely supports reassurance, confidence, convenience, identity or another end value.

## Message hierarchy
Use priority as attention authority, not as a fixed number of assets:
- **P0**: product identity + one Primary Conversion Proposition.
- **P1**: relevance / fit / secondary reasons.
- **P2**: reasons-to-believe / proof / facts.
- **P3**: trust, objection reduction, expectation setting.
- **ACTION**: offer/CTA only when platform and economics justify it.
- **POST-PURCHASE**: usage and expectation management when needed.

## Belief Sequence
Design the shopper state transition:
1. IDENTIFY — What is it?
2. RELATE — Why is it relevant to me?
3. BELIEVE — Why should I believe it?
4. REDUCE RISK — Will I buy the wrong thing / misuse it / regret it?
5. ACT — What is the natural next action?
6. USE / RETAIN — How do I achieve the intended experience after purchase?

Do not force every surface to contain every step. Each surface should move the shopper one useful step forward.

## Cognitive Budget
Mobile attention is scarce.
- One dominant proposition in the first impression.
- Identity and necessary specification may coexist but should not compete visually with the proposition.
- Proof comes when it resolves disbelief, not because “proof cards” are part of a template.

## Signaling
Use visual and verbal signals only when they are backed by the actual product and desired perception.
- Packaging fidelity, material/finish, evidence strength, photography precision and language quality can signal trust.
- Laboratory, doctor, luxury, natural, local-culture or creator symbols cannot substitute for evidence.

## Decision contract
Every major positioning/message choice uses:
`WHAT → WHY → EVIDENCE → CONSUMER LOGIC → PLATFORM LOGIC → CULTURE LOGIC → BUSINESS LOGIC → EXECUTION → MEASUREMENT`.

## v0.5.13 Host strategy synthesis and semantic separation
The deterministic runtime may begin with a fact-led proposition. The Host/model must turn that baseline into commercial meaning without inventing stronger claims.
- P0 is the primary reason to choose; P1 adds a distinct relevance/fit reason; P2 is a distinct reason-to-believe or proof condition; P3 reduces a concrete risk.
- Do not repeat the same claim, number or sentence as both proposition and proof. If no distinct approved proof exists, omit P2 or state the evidence task instead of duplicating P0/P1.
- Translate supported facts through `Fact → Advantage/RTB → Perceived Benefit → justified emotional/end value`; stop whenever evidence does not support the next layer.
- A good Primary Conversion Proposition should answer why the fact matters to the current buyer decision, not merely restate a specification.
- Preserve important trade-offs and risk reducers. A stronger value proposition does not hide weight, cleaning burden, source boundary, fit constraint, weather limit or study condition.
